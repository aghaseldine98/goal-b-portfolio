What changed since last time, and why

The decision of 2026-11-13 (research to the 2026-11-12 close, gb-decision-16) takes effect at the next US open.

This week I made a few small changes rather than a rebuild.

What I sold: I sold the whole Intuit holding, about 5.5% of the portfolio. Intuit, the maker of TurboTax and QuickBooks, is still a good business. But it had the lowest expected return in the portfolio, about 8.4% a year at today's price against about 7.6% for the market as a whole. Analysts' profit forecasts for it have been edging down, and the shares fell by about half over the year to last month. It also tends to move with our Adobe shares, so it was doubling a bet we already make.

I also cut Nvidia from about 11% to 9%. Its chief executive, Jensen Huang, resigned on 11 November, and the company has an interim leader while the board searches. News reports also describe a government probe. The business was selling about $96 billion of products a quarter at its last report, and I still expect about 11% a year from the shares. But until the leadership and legal questions are settled, I don't want it to be our biggest holding, and it had drifted above my 10% limit anyway.

What I bought: a new 6% position in Jabil, a contract manufacturer that now builds much of the hardware inside AI data centres. It expects $44.5 billion of revenue this year, up 24%, and analysts have been raising their forecasts. On the researcher's cautious assumptions the shares offer about 8.9% a year. I also added a little to Sanmina, a similar manufacturer where I expect about 10.4% a year, and to Crocs, the footwear brand, where I expect about 11.7% a year.

Overall, the portfolio's expected return on the same measure is about 11.1% a year, against about 7.6% for the market. These are estimates, not promises. Nvidia, Gap, Oracle and Jabil all report in the next few weeks, and I will check each against its numbers.

CROX

Buybacks cutting shares about 9% a year make per-share earnings compound faster than operating income. The main risks are NA Crocs weakness, a HEYDUDE relapse, oil-driven costs and tariff policy.
  • Wrong if: Brent crude front-month price Above $115/bbl for 30 consecutive trading days (by 2027-06-30); Q4 2026 reported revenue growth YoY (Q4 release, around Feb 2027) Below +5% (guided +7-9%) (by 2027-03-15); H1 2027 GAAP operating margin (Q1+Q2 2027 combined) Below 21.5% (base implies about 23.9%) (by 2027-08-31); TTM revenue at 2027-09-30 Below $4.15B (base $4.28B) (by 2027-11-30); Crocs Brand North America revenue YoY in Q1 or Q2 2027 Decline worse than -6% (by 2027-08-31)
