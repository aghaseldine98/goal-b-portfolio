What changed since last time, and why

The decision of 2026-11-13 (research to the 2026-11-12 close, gb-decision-16) takes effect at the next US open.

This week I made five changes.

I sold all of Intuit, the maker of TurboTax and QuickBooks. It is a good business, but at today's price I expected only about 8.4% a year from it, the least of any regular holding. Every sign of momentum was poor. Analysts have been trimming their forecasts. The shares fell after its last results. They lost about half their value in the year to last month. Intuit also tends to move with Adobe, which I keep because I expect more from it (about 11.9%).

I also cut Nvidia from about 11% to 7.5%. Part of that was required, because no holding may exceed 10%. The rest is caution. On 12 November Nvidia told regulators it is changing its chief executive. I could not open the filing, so I do not know why, and the research behind the holding never considered it. Demand for its AI chips still looks strong, and the shares already sit near their low for the year at about $166. So I am holding less, not selling out, until its next results and the reasons are clear.

With the money I did three things. First, I bought a small new holding in Cheniere Energy, the largest US exporter of liquefied natural gas. About 95% of its output is sold on long contracts that pay a fixed fee, and analysts have raised their profit forecasts sharply in the last six weeks. I expect only about 7.4% a year, a little under the market's 7.6%, because some of today's profit comes from unusually high world gas prices that should fade. I hold it for the improving forecasts and because it has not moved in step with anything else we own. It is 4.5% of the portfolio.

Second, I added to Lear, the car-seat and wiring maker, taking it to 9.3%. Oil has fallen from about $107 to $92 a barrel since the research was written, which eases the main risk to car production. The share price still assumes profit margins less than half the company's typical level. I expect about 13% a year.

Third, I added to Sanmina, which builds AI server racks, taking it to 6.8%. It has guided next year's sales to $17.5-19.5 billion, and I expect about 10.4% a year.

Overall I expect the portfolio to return about 11.0% a year, against about 7.6% for the market. It holds 12 companies, none above 10%.
