What changed since last time, and why

The decision of 2026-11-11 (research to the 2026-11-10 close, gb-decision-14) was decided on 2026-11-11 and is the current book.

This week I made one real change and a few small top-ups.

I sold all of Netflix. It is an excellent business, but at about $63 a share the price already matches what its research supports (about $62 a share), so the yearly return I expect from here, about 8.1%, is barely above the roughly 7.5% the whole market offers. Nothing went wrong with the company; the money simply works harder elsewhere.

I bought a small 4.5% stake in Sanmina, a contract manufacturer that, since buying ZT Systems, assembles complete AI server racks for the big cloud companies. It expects next year's revenue of $17.5 to $19.5 billion and adjusted earnings of $15.50 to $17.50 a share; at $155 the stock costs about 9 to 10 times that, after falling about 27% in the past month. I expect about 10.7% a year. I kept it small because it moves roughly two and a half times as much as the market and a handful of customers buy most of what it makes.

The rest of the Netflix money raised six existing holdings to just under my 10% limit: Regeneron, Tenet, Adobe, PayPal, Crocs and Gap.

I also lowered four of my own estimates where the standard calculation used an input I do not trust: Oracle to 9.0% (it left out about $129.5 billion of debt), Tenet to 9.8% (part of its profit belongs to its surgery-centre partners), and Crocs and PayPal to 11.7% each (higher tax rates than assumed).

Expected yearly returns now, against about 7.5% for the market: Gap 15.1%, Lear 12.9%, Adobe 12.0%, Crocs 11.7%, PayPal 11.7%, Nvidia 10.9%, Sanmina 10.7%, Tenet 9.8%, Regeneron 9.6%, Oracle 9.0%, Microsoft 8.4%, Intuit 8.3%. Together about 11.0%. Intuit is the lowest; I keep it because its year is fully forecast by the company and the nearest alternative is only a tenth of a point better. These are estimates from research, not promises, and the next tests are Nvidia's and Gap's results in the coming weeks.
