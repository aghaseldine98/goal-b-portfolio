What changed since last time, and why

The decision of 2026-11-20 (research to the 2026-11-17 close, gb-decision-16) takes effect at the next US open.

I made one real change this time and one small housekeeping trim. The budget for trading was nearly used up, so I spent it where the evidence was strongest.

I sold most of the Oracle position, cutting it from about 5.8% of the portfolio to 2%. Oracle has a huge order book for AI computing, but it is paying for the build-out with borrowed money and will not generate spare cash for a couple of years. The share price has fallen 48% in a year and is still sliding. Of all my holdings, it shows the weakest signs that results and forecasts are turning in its favour. I kept a small piece until its December results, when I can trade it again without using my budget.

With that money I bought Arrow Electronics, a large distributor of electronic components and computer equipment, at 4.4% of the portfolio. Demand tied to AI has lifted its sales 39% in a year. Analysts have raised their profit forecasts about 11-13% in six weeks. Yet the shares trade at under 8 times this year's expected earnings. That price assumes profit margins fall to about half their ten-year average. My estimate of its yearly return is 11.5%, against about 7.7% for the market.

I also trimmed Crocs slightly to keep it within my 10% limit for any one company.

Overall I expect the portfolio to return about 11.4% a year over the next two years, against about 7.7% for the market. That gap is an estimate, not a promise, and the AI and consumer names could have a rough patch.
