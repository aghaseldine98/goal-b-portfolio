The agent's note (decided 2026-11-08)

This week I made a large rotation. I sold Amazon, Broadcom, AppLovin, Booking, TTM Technologies, Micron, EMCOR and our index fund. None of these businesses broke; the issue is price. On our own research, each now offers a yearly return at or below the roughly 7.5% the whole market offers (Amazon about 6.2%, Booking 6.5%, AppLovin 7.0%, Broadcom 7.2%, EMCOR 7.8%, TTM 4.5%), and Micron's 8.5% leans on peak memory-chip profits lasting forever. The index fund simply earns the market's 7.5%.

I bought five companies. Gap, the clothing retailer (expected about 15.4% a year): the shares cost about 9 times this year's expected earnings and the company holds about $1 billion more cash than debt. Crocs (13.3%): it just raised its profit outlook for the third time this year and the share price is about 10.4 times that outlook. Lear, the car-seat maker (14.2%): a stock-market value of about $5.6 billion against $565-665 million of expected free cash this year; car production is the risk, so it is a smaller holding. Tenet, hospitals and surgery centres (11.5%): about 12.6 times this year's guided earnings after a raised outlook; Medicaid changes from 2027 are the risk. Oracle (10.1%): its cloud contracts total $664 billion, but the build-out is debt-funded, so I kept it to 6%.

I added to PayPal (12.3%), Adobe (11.9%) and Regeneron (9.6%), kept Nvidia (10.2%) and Microsoft (8.4%), and trimmed Netflix (8.1%) and Intuit (8.3%).

Added up, the portfolio's expected return is about 11.3% a year against about 7.5% for the market and 4.3% for cash. The trade-off is real: we now own much less of the AI chip boom. If those stocks keep surging we will lag, and Gap, Crocs and Lear would all suffer in a consumer slowdown.

Exposures taken on purpose

Each is a measured tilt or stress result the agent saw and chose to accept, reduce or hedge.

  • market beta (market beta, accept): Measured beta 0.66 covers only 78% of weight (CROX, LEA, ORCL missing; CROX Blume beta 1.18, LEA 0.95), so true beta is higher, plausibly ~0.85-0.9. The low figure comes from idiosyncratic holdings (REGN, NFLX, ADBE, INTU with IWB correlations 0.05-0.12), not cash; the book is fully invested. I accept lagging a strong broad rally as the cost of owning the names with the best evidence.
  • ai proxy (driver, accept): -3.03% per +10% semis beyond the market (t -11.93) is the deliberate valuation call: the AI names sold are priced for y2 growth of ~55% or more on their own studies. The bet is bounded by keeping NVDA (9.8%), ORCL (6%) and MSFT (8%). This is not a forecast of an AI slowdown.
  • ai capex pause (scenario, accept): +11.62% vs IWB in the stress, mostly the same ai_proxy bet plus low measured beta; the beta part is overstated for the reasons above. It is the mirror of the AI-underweight risk I accept, not a reason for the position.
  • rates up (scenario, accept): +3.37% comes entirely from measured beta; no reliable rate sensitivity (t 0.42). On a truer ~0.9 beta the cushion is ~1%. Levered holdings (THC, ORCL, LEA) would suffer from wider credit; I monitor the HY spread instead of hedging.
  • recession (scenario, accept): +11.46% is beta (8.43) plus ai_proxy (3.03), but the regression omits CROX and LEA, and the consumer cluster (25.5%) would be hurt in a recession, so the real cushion is smaller.
